# Taste
- Works in discrete phases and issues a single terse directive naming the phase (e.g. "start phase 1, complete it", "start executing phase 2") expecting the agent to drive the whole phase to done — implement, verify with build/lint, update the progress log, and commit — before reporting back, with no further input from him. Confidence: 0.65
- Wants a concrete, hands-on checklist of what he can personally see/test after a unit of work (what to click, what to expect, and what is deliberately not implemented yet) — not just a summary of changes. Confidence: 0.55
- Prefers terse, to-the-point answers with no preamble — asks for things "in short" / "to the point". Confidence: 0.55
- Wants periodic progress/status updates during long-running work rather than silence — when he loses visibility into what's happening he assumes nothing is being done and may terminate the run. Confidence: 0.7
- Is time- and token-cost conscious; pushes back when a task consumes a lot of time/tokens without visible progress. Confidence: 0.6
- Prefers to verify rendered UI/visual behavior manually himself rather than have the agent install extra global tooling (e.g. browser-automation packages) for verification. Confidence: 0.5
- Wants blockers (e.g. assets that don't work) reported back to him instead of the agent silently improvising a workaround or substitute — he then decides which alternative to take; states this emphatically ("Do NOT silently substitute assets or work around visual problems"). Confidence: 0.8
- Insists the real backend/system state be the single source of truth: derived visuals/logic must come from actual state values, never hardcoded per-id special cases or a second fake/frontend simulation built just to demo the effect. Confidence: 0.8
- Won't accept bare completion claims ("do not just tell me 'P4 is complete'") — expects concrete evidence that a change caused the stated effect (measured values, before/after behavior), not assertions. Confidence: 0.7
- Cares about rendering/runtime performance and expects it preserved: hold the stated FPS target, avoid expensive per-frame re-renders, keep effects lightweight, and don't rebuild/recreate the scene on each data update. Confidence: 0.6
